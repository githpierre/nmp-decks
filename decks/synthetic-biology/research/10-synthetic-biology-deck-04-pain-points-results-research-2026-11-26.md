note: We treat these as problems that new tools and services have removed for most teams. They do not mean that products are now cheap to make at commercial scale.

  5: Writing a gene took weeks of manual cloning in each research lab
  7: Companies like Twist now print DNA on silicon chips, sell it online and ship it within days to labs around the world
  8: Long DNA pieces had to be assembled by hand in many slow steps
  10: Ansa now sells sequence-checked DNA up to 50,000 letters long, with a guaranteed shipping date for each order
  11: Finding a better protein meant screening thousands of random versions
  13: AI models such as Profluent's or Cradle's propose designs that work in fewer lab rounds, cutting months of tests
  14: Every startup had to build its own lab robots and data systems
  16: Biofoundries and cloud labs rent automated build and test runs by project, so startups skip buying robots
  17: Food proteins made by engineered microbes had no clear US path
  19: The FDA has cleared whey, casein and egg proteins made by fermentation, from Perfect Day in 2020 to Formo in 2026
  20: Each new company had to build its own fermentation plant
  22: Contract makers and shared pilot plants now rent tank time to startups, as Formo and Standing Ovation do in 2026
