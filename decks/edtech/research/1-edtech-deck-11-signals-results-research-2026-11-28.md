## Format screening and limitations

- **Kept:** key-trend (2), icon-grid (2), fallen-star (1), deal-timeline (1), kpi-trio (2), country-gap (1), multiple-gap (1), profit-gap (1), funnel (1). The latest 2026 figures are fresh for opportunity candidates; the 2025 Eurostat data is retained for structural comparison only. Khanmigo's research is a published cumulative study result, not a current usage estimate.
- **Not retained:** rules-wave (education-specific three-year counts surfaced but no fourth comparable year); waiting-line (no verified comparable waiting/served counts); incident-count (no verified comparable official annual series); announced-vs-built (no sufficiently precise matched plan and actual); idle-capacity (institution-level available tools versus individual use is not a valid capacity comparison); promise-tracker (no three verified slips); lobbying-spend (no verified LD-2 series); cost-threshold (no 4-10 comparable prices and reference); penetration-curve (available general AI surveys include non-EdTech tools and lacked an appropriate earlier-technology comparison); adoption-race (no sufficiently verified comparable dated milestones); earnings-mentions (no verified EdTech-specific series); real-world-vs-funding and funding-event (internal funding database not provided, excluded by instruction); incumbent-crash (Chegg's peak comparison is more defensible than an unverified quarterly event series); challenger (no matched private/public revenue trajectory); dominance (no comparable education-specific market share series); contract-share (no validated set of public award amounts); revenue-per-head (no robust matched headcount/revenue dataset checked); dollar-split (no published comparable breakdown); single-customer (no verified concentration of at least 30%); forecast-gap (no five comparable same-year forecasts verified); value-ladder (mixed market caps and private valuations or noneligible incumbents would weaken the result).
- This is a screened research shortlist, not an exhaustive audit of every possible source for every format. Quotes from tables or long sentences are short verbatim excerpts; table separators indicate source rows. The six opportunity candidates include two alternatives from the same Coursera report and one cumulative distribution proof, rather than six independent acceleration measures. For a final deck, prefer the first Coursera candidate and avoid duplicating the GenAI enrollment rate.
