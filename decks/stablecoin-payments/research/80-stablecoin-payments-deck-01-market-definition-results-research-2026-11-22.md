page: 2/2
note: We focused on myths that come back often in investor, banker and press debates. The counters describe what laws, companies and incidents have shown so far.
t:
  9: Stablecoin payments are always cheaper
  7: Fees to turn local money into coins and back, plus compliance costs, add up, so a small transfer can cost about as much as a classic money transfer
  12: Stablecoin payments are anonymous and hidden
  10: Payment firms must check identity and screen transfers like banks, and issuers like Tether and Circle can freeze coins linked to crime or sanctions
  15: Only crypto startups build this market
  13: Stripe paid $1.1B for Bridge in 2025, PayPal issues its own PYUSD coin, and Visa and Mastercard now link their cards to stablecoin balances
  0: Stablecoins are a fad that will fade away
  16: Total stablecoin supply passed $250B in 2025, about double its early 2024 level, and the US, EU and Hong Kong now regulate them as payment money

- d: SINCE 2025

  t: The US GENIUS Act became law in July 2025, the first federal law for payment stablecoins, requiring full reserves in cash or short-term Treasury bills.
